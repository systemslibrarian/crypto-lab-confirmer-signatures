# Confirmer Signatures — Crypto Lab (Research Gate)

**Research status:** Chapter 01's **ordinary P-256 ECDSA baseline is implemented and tested**. There is **no executable designated confirmer signature**, and no completed DCS site, confirmation, disavowal or catalog card is approved.

**Planned visitor experience: _Signature Gate — Who Gets to Verify?_** Alice signs a fictional confidential agreement, Carol provides an interactive mathematical confirmation or disavowal, Bob evaluates the proof, and Dave illustrates when evidence is publicly transferable. The user should see why ordinary signature validity, controlled verification, and truth of a signed statement are separate properties.

**Research progress:** Fubiao Xia's [2013 doctoral thesis](https://etheses.bham.ac.uk/4579/1/Xia13PhD.pdf), a closely related full-verification DCS construction by a coauthor of the 2016 paper, supplies explicit labeled-encryption, confirmation and disavowal formulas. We have implemented and locally tested the **intentionally insecure n=253 Camenisch–Shoup-style labeled encryption arithmetic** in [research/cs-paillier.mjs](research/cs-paillier.mjs). The new [research-only π0 equality subproof](research/pi0-equality.mjs) implements the displayed Fiat–Shamir response/recomputation relation from thesis §6.5.2 over the same trivially factorable toy group. Its independent tests also demonstrate that the subrelation alone **does not bind a message commitment ψ or verify the complete ciphertext label/tag**. See [research/PI0-RELATION.md](research/PI0-RELATION.md) and [research/THESIS-EQUATIONS.md](research/THESIS-EQUATIONS.md) for exact formulas and blockers. The new [toy π1 witness-relation checker](research/pi1-relation.mjs) evaluates seven separate source-defined arithmetic equations, plus independently checks a labeled decrypt; its arguments **include the private witness** and therefore it is **not a proof**, interactive confirmation, or DCS. See [research/PI1-WITNESS-RELATION.md](research/PI1-WITNESS-RELATION.md) for the exact boundaries. The [four-branch research-only disavowal inspector](research/disavow-branches.mjs) evaluates the four invalidity predicates outlined in Camenisch–Shoup §7.3, including the important difference between a malformed ciphertext and an affirmative invalidity proof. It is handed the decryption key and does **not** generate an interactive OR proof, exact interval proof, or non-transferable transcript. The [primary-source analysis](research/DISAVOWAL-BRANCHES.md) documents why those are required. Original research suites covered **49 tests** (9 ECDSA baseline, 8 labeled-encryption, 12 toy π0, 9 toy π1 witness relations, 11 disavowal diagnostics). Later three-move and special-soundness suites increase the enforced current count to **at least 77 tests**; see GitHub Actions for the executed count. **This is not a DCS implementation or a complete cryptographic π0 proof.**

**New arithmetic milestone:** [research/pi1-interactive-toy.mjs](research/pi1-interactive-toy.mjs) implements a thesis-derived three-move *toy* π1/π2 relation: seven equations checked by a public-only verifier after a fresh two-bit challenge. [The limitation report](research/PI1-TRANSCRIPT.md) explicitly demonstrates why accepting those equations is **not a designated-confirmer signature verification**, including a zero-challenge counterexample and replay. The source's strict range condition, a concurrent-ZK compiler, a full issuance certificate, and affirmative disavowal remain unimplemented. **The visitor-facing Chapter 02 stays locked.**

**Two-challenge research finding:** [pi1-special-soundness.mjs](research/pi1-special-soundness.mjs) demonstrates that reusing the SAME proof commitment with different verifier challenges lets an observer recover the toy decryption key exponents and hidden opening from the public response differences. [Documented negative controls](research/PI1-SPECIAL-SOUNDNESS.md) include fresh-mask behavior, challenge validation and misleading zero-challenge acceptance. This teaches a genuine algebraic failure mode but is NOT evidence of a completed DCS protocol. None of this research Node code is included in the ordinary-signature web preview.

This repository records the development brief and the cryptographic selection gate for a proposed interactive lab on designated confirmer signatures. Genuine confirmation **and disavowal** protocols must come from a reviewed, published construction and pass independent mathematical checks. A normal signature hidden by encryption is not automatically a designated confirmer signature.
