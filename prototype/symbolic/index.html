Signature Gate / Research experiment 02

Can Carol prove Alice signed it?

Alice signs a fictional agreement. Bob wants proof it is valid. Carol is the designated confirmer. Watch the signer and confirmer each use actual mathematical equations to answer Bob's challenge.

RESEARCH ONLY — THIS IS NOT SECURE CRYPTOGRAPHY OR A FINISHED DESIGNATED CONFIRMER SIGNATURE. The group here is a symbolic teaching model: its public numbers expose the private exponents, so secrecy, zero knowledge and non-transferability do not hold. We are reproducing the algebra of Xia's 2013 Chapter 5 source, which is a different construction from the 2016 scheme still under review.

03 / What's still missing?

Rejecting a proof is not proving a signature was forged

The published scheme also requires an affirmative disavowal proof. This experiment does not implement disavowal. When Alice or Carol cannot prove validity, Bob has learned nothing conclusive about invalidity. We will not substitute a status button for the missing proof.

Inspect the source equations and security limits
  • Source: Fubiao Xia, 2013 thesis, §5.5 and Figures 5.1–5.4. The actual issuing structure is signed ElGamal over BLS signatures; OR confirmation is shown in Figures 5.1 and 5.2.
  • The symbolic pairing computes exponent products modulo 1019. Everybody can see those exponents. It cannot protect a signing key.
  • Confirmation tests a mathematical relation. It does not check whether the real-world words in Alice's agreement are true.
  • Disavowal Figures 5.3/5.4 require further independent checking. As transcribed, a displayed non-identity ratio appears to be the identity by the source's own public-key definition. The workbench does not invent a correction.
  • The 2013 OR proof alone does not establish concurrent zero knowledge or transferability restrictions; those need the published compiler and its formal security proof.
